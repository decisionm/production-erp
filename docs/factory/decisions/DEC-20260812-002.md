Purchase orders are raised in the ERP from now on, not in Tally, and the ERP sends each one to Tally as a Purchase Order voucher. One book of purchase orders, not two. This completes the direction already true for goods receipts: the ERP raises, Tally records. The consequence, stated plainly: the accountant stops keying purchase orders into Tally, and a purchase order keyed directly into Tally after the cutover would be invisible to the ERP's remaining-quantity tracking, so the two books would stop agreeing. The build that follows must post a Tally Purchase Order voucher through an enqueuePurchaseOrder() path shaped like the existing Receipt Note path — vendor resolved to its Tally ledger, items to their Tally stock items, nothing hardcoded. It is an ORDER voucher and must not touch accounts or stock in Tally; that is to be stated in the code and proved by a test. Where a vendor or an item has no Tally identity the ERP refuses to post rather than guessing, surfaced the way unmapped names are surfaced today. The existing PurchaseOrder columns are used honestly: source records where the order was raised, tally_order_no records what Tally accepted it as, and neither is invented. Nothing changes on the day this is recorded; the build comes after the owner's meeting and the goods-receipt fix. Five questions are deliberately left open for the owner and the accountant rather than assumed: the cutover date, whether open orders already in Tally finish there or move to the ERP, whose purchase-order NUMBER is authoritative, whether the accountant wants an order voucher in Tally at all, and whether a Tally Purchase Order voucher needs the Purchase ledger mapped or only the vendor ledger.
