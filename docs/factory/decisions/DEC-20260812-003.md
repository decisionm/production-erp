Purchase and tax settings are CONFIGURATION, not code: GST rates come from the government and change, so once set they are held as data a person maintains, the way production already has a configuration. Three parts. (1) The company's own state decides local versus interstate — proven by the 12-Aug Day Book, where 90 of 92 vouchers follow Local ledger with a Puducherry party and CGST+SGST, Interstate with any other state and IGST. (2) The purchase ledger is chosen per (local or interstate) x rate, because Tally actually uses FOUR purchase ledgers and a single Purchase mapping cannot express that. (3) GST RATES MUST BE EFFECTIVE-DATED, and this is the load-bearing part: the owner's own export proves the same items were booked at 5 percent and at 18 percent in different months, so one rate per item or per HSN would misprice either the history or the present. A rate is held as 'this rate, from this date', the same shape as a superseding decision record — a new rate never edits the old one, and a voucher dated in April resolves to April's rate. Vendor payment terms and delivery defaults belong to the vendor they describe, not to a global setting, because the data shows them varying per vendor (45 days on 51 of 92 orders, 60 on 17, also 30 and 15). NO TAX RATE IS EVER SEEDED AS A DEFAULT. Leave days were seeded as a starting point because a wrong leave figure is corrected on review; a wrong tax rate is money. The container is built and left empty, and the accountant — who is accountable for it — decides which item is which rate. That question is open and explicitly not the agent's: published Indian GST rates put paper and cardboard packaging at 5 percent since 22-Sep-2025 while plastic packaging stays 18 percent, which would make the split appear in every month, yet the data shows 5 percent only in April, May and June, and the owner understands the recent change as everything now being 18 percent. Those readings disagree, so the configuration must be able to hold BOTH rates and the accountant resolves which applies.
