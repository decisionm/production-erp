Configuration masters carry a real hard delete, but only for records proven never-used and dependency-free; everything else is Archive. Five rules govern it. (1) Hard delete is permitted only where the record has never been used and nothing depends on it. (2) An ARCHIVED record retains and reserves its business code: historical codes are never reused, so a retired code stays taken. A genuinely unused record that is hard-deleted may release its code, because nothing in history ever referred to it. (3) Hard delete is Super Admin / Owner level only; other permitted configuration users may create, edit, activate and deactivate according to RBAC. (4) Archiving or deactivating in the ERP causes NO automatic Tally mutation: historical Tally identity and mapping are preserved, and any change to a Tally master is a separate, explicit action. (5) 'Ever used' means historical references, maintenance schedules and every other dependency, not merely current foreign keys; audit evidence is built or preserved where needed, and where historical use cannot be safely proven for legacy data the system REFUSES the hard delete and offers Archive only. Destructive cascade must never be used to make a dependency check pass.
